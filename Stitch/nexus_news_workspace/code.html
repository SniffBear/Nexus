[WASHINGTON] The Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) decided unanimously on Wednesday to maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at 5.25% to 5.50%. The rate decision reflects persistent caution among central bank policymakers following uneven readings in core personal consumption expenditures (PCE) through the first half of the year.

"The Committee does not expect it will be appropriate to reduce the target range until it has gained greater confidence that inflation is moving sustainably toward 2 percent. Recent indicators suggest that economic activity has continued to expand at a solid pace, though job gains have moderated."

The updated Summary of Economic Projections (SEP) reveals a pivotal shift in the committee's forward path. The median dot plot now pencils in just one 25-basis-point reduction before year-end, down sharply from the three cuts anticipated during the March projections. Four participants indicated they foresee no rate reductions whatsoever through the calendar year 2024.

During his post-meeting press conference, Chair Jerome Powell noted that while recent consumer price data showed welcoming normalization, policymakers require a string of consistently benign data points before loosening credit conditions. The Fed also confirmed it will continue reducing its holdings of Treasury securities and agency debt as previously outlined.
